# CR-ALP-058 quadrature enclosure: forward-error justification

The saved `validated_1.log`, `validated_2.log` are generated by `validated.cpp` using binary128 outward-rounded interval arithmetic and MPFR elementary functions. The point evaluator uses a midpoint fast path with a deliberately conservative absolute padding; this is a classical forward-error proof, not an independent interval quadrature reconstruction. Assume IEEE binary128 round-to-nearest basic arithmetic (no fast-math), correctly rounded MPFR 192-bit operations and directed conversion as in the code. Replay on GNU/Linux x86-64 with `g++ -O2 -std=gnu++17 validated.cpp -o validated -lquadmath -l:libmpfr.so.6 -lgmp`. The C ABI declarations are used because the development header is absent in this environment. The MPFR library itself is not bundled.

## Exact inputs and mesh

`prepare.py` checks the three predecessor bank hashes, contracts integer midpoints with the frozen rational witness, checks the frozen witness bank hash and its K interval, and removes only groups at M>=2^32. The total L2 contribution of a reflected Poisson primitive e^-ct (ct)^r/r! is <=2/sqrt(2c), by integrating its square on the half-line and using binom(2r,r)<=4^r. This proves the reported discarded-group bounds. Q85 is contractive. `validate_geometry.py` independently checks the actual Gauss root brackets, weights, physical coefficient budgets, maximal group center and rational error budgets. `mesh.py` regenerated the saved mesh byte-for-byte. It approximates each log(n) by an exact rational displacement to error <1e-70, enclosing its actual prime weight via the log series and integer sqrt. Its panels cover exactly half each overlap by symmetry and lie in fixed analytic pieces. The rational centers and halfwidths are outward converted in the evaluator. The product of two same-parity functions at t and t+shift is invariant under t -> d-shift-t; this also holds for projection products against same-parity sine/pole functions, so double the half-domain rule. Endpoint values at jumps do not affect integrals.

## Whole-panel ideal quadrature error

On each panel with halfwidth h<=1/128 use the complex disk of radius R=4h. The mesh asserts either the real evaluation center and its reflected center are >=2R from endpoints, or c_max R<=1/2, for both shifted arguments. For a reflected grouped primitive, if the center y>=2R, then |z|<=3 Re z on the disk (z=c times the complex argument). Hence |e^-z z^r/r!|<=3^r by the Poisson bound. If c_max R<=1/2, then |z|-Re z<=1, and the bound is e. The sum of absolute grouped coefficients is <1000 (r<=39). Explicit low boundary coefficients sum to <1e14, beta<256; their reflected contributions on the disk are <2*1e14*exp(8). Ordinary sine/cosine piece coefficient sum is <1e17 and maximal frequency <850*(10/7), so each complex sine/cosine magnitude <=exp(850*(10/7)/32)<1e17. Both pole exponential piece coefficients have magnitude <1e17, with bounded exponential factors (<4). The separate resonant convolution has f<100 and elementary exponential-linear terms of bounded magnitude. These inequalities, including after projection, give the uniform complex bound 1e40 per function. Normalized sine and pole factors used for projection also satisfy it. Thus every integrated product is bounded by 1e80 on the disk. For degree 191 Taylor truncation, Cauchy gives remainder <= (4/3)*1e80/4^192 on the real panel. The 96-node Gauss rule is exact on degree 191; positivity and sum of weights 2 give total quadrature error <=4*d*(4/3)*1e80/4^192 <1e-12 even after reflection. This direct product estimate replaces the older mesh diagnostic involving an unverified real-axis bound of 100; that diagnostic is not needed for acceptance. The C++ pads each integral by 1e-12, vastly exceeding the ideal error.

## Midpoint point evaluation

All interval basic operations enclose the exact operation by a nextafter in the required direction. Input and weight endpoints use directed MPFR conversion; the monotone exponentials and square roots use directed MPFR operations; sine/cosine intervals use the 1-Lipschitz bound from their midpoint. The fast midpoint evaluator uses correctly rounded MPFR values rounded to binary128 (allow two binary128 ulps for the double rounding), and binary128 arithmetic. The code checks time interval radius <1e-31, frequency and sqrt(a) radii <1e-32, group centers radius <1e-20, c<2^34, low amplitude sum <1e14, group amplitude sum <1e3 and piece coefficient sums/magnitudes as above. Use conservative arithmetic unit 2^-112.

* At the restarted sine phases, including t and nu input uncertainty, angle error is < 850*(2.2*1e-32+(10/7)*1e-31)+1e-29 < 2e-28. The step angle error is < 4e-31. At most 15 exact-plane-rotation steps with the rounded sin/cos step, each with component arithmetic error < 20*2^-112, give a vector error <1e-27 (the rotation norm is at most 1 plus its step error). Thus the sine/cosine contribution error is <1e-10. The normalized basis point padding 1e-26 suffices.
* For the low boundary exponentials the explicit and reflected time/exponent roundoff is bounded by <1e-27 for each value, including at most 127 multiplications; times 2*1e14 gives <2e-13. The grouped Poisson primitive has derivative in its real argument bounded by 2, since it is a difference of two Poisson terms (r=0 separately). The argument error < 3e-20+2^34*2e-31+1e-24 < 4e-20; with coefficient sum 1e3 and reflection, this contributes <2e-16. If x>1000 the primitive with r<=39 is negligible (<1e-300), also after argument uncertainty. Otherwise recurrence starts with correctly rounded exp(-x), has <=39 multiplications/divisions and its exact term magnitude <=1; even a 200*2^-112 relative budget is negligible here. The exp(-x) arguments are in a range representable by binary128 (no overflow). Very small intermediate terms, if underflowed, have negligible absolute effect.
* Exponential piece and resonant terms have bounded argument error <1e-30, exponential magnitude <4, and total coefficient magnitude <=2e17 plus bounded resonant factors. Their error <1e-11. The resonant coefficient and pole parameter conversion errors are also covered by this budget. Coefficient interval radii are separately accumulated with reflected basis magnitudes <=2 and pole exponential magnitudes <4.
* The evaluation comprises fewer than 5000 scalar additions/multiplications per point outside the elementary-function and recurrence calculations just bounded; each sum partial magnitude <2e18, so their aggregate summation rounding allowance 5000*2e18*2^-112 <2e-12. Taken together the arithmetic and argument errors are <1e-9, below the 1e-8 midpoint padding in `midpointize`. The latter is in addition to the explicit coefficient interval radii. All interval products and sums of quadrature evaluations are then outward, with exact-root and positive-weight intervals.

The first projection integral encloses the ordinary sine and pole pairings. The evaluator forms st=||b||^2-sum c_i^2 and verifies st>0.003, then lambda=(<raw,b>-sum c_i <raw,e_i>)/st. Subtracting the low sine projection and lambda R85 b implements the complete Q85 projector. The second point evaluation includes those interval coefficient radii; the max radius increases to about 5.1e-5 in the saved logs. No numerical assumption that raw projection cancels exactly is made in the evaluation.

## Actual shifts and proxy transport

For the retained real pieces (before and after projection) the same coefficient bounds give uniform amplitude <1e18 and derivative <1e21 off finitely many knots: sine derivative costs at most 850*(10/7); low boundary derivative costs <256; grouped derivatives cost <=2c per primitive. Unshifted sine/pole source and trial sums also satisfy these bounds, checked from the actual input. Extend by zero and use at most 32 jump neighborhoods in a displacement. For |delta|<=1e-70 the L2 translation error is bounded by 2*1e21*|delta|+2*1e18*sqrt(32|delta|). The weight sum of the paired shifts is <8, so the total physical proxy displacement error is <1e-13, also after Q85. The signed prime moment evaluated with rational shifts rather than exact shifts has an additional discrepancy <1e-13: use the corresponding L1 displacement bound times the amplitude bound and paired weight sum. The exact checker budgets both allowances separately. The frozen witness has squared norm <1.000001; `check_saved.py` verifies its precise norm, and transports the inherited 5e-9 proxy error with a rational upper bound on 5e-9*||z||. The discarded groups are included as additional L2 error. The integrated projected proxy norm is <0.4. For total L2 error eps, the squared norm discrepancy is <=0.8 eps+eps^2, and the signed prime moment discrepancy is <=(21/8)(0.8 eps+eps^2), plus the shift allowance. Exact endpoint calculations establish the affine falsifier with ample slack. This relies on inherited classical continuum identities, the CR-ALP-057 proxy-error proof and the described arithmetic assumptions; it is not a Lean proof or independently regenerated global coefficient bank.
