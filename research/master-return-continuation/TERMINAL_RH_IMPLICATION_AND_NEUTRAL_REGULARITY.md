## 2. Direct implication from unrestricted positivity to RH
Work with shifted actual zeros z=ρ-1/2, indexed by distinct strip zeros with positive analytic multiplicity m_z. The involution z^#=-conj(z) preserves the divisor and multiplicity. Each has |Re z|<1/2; every bounded ordinate interval has finitely many zeros by the pinned count. Write F_f(z)=integral f(x)exp(zx) dx. On compact supported logarithmic vectors, the inherited full-divisor sampling estimate gives sum_z m_z|F_f(z)|²<infinity; the reflected family has the same bound. Define the conjugate-linear-first Hermitian pairing
Q(f,g)=sum_z m_z conj(F_f(z^#)) F_g(z).
Absolute convergence by Cauchy-Schwarz. Hermitian symmetry by reflection permutation. On diagonal it is real and equals the original graph quadratic by exact raw-divisor identity; on smooth core equals original arithmetic form via literal EF, extended to H_a by the prior attachment. No auxiliary positivity or sample interpolation assumed.

Suppose there is an off-line zero. Reflection supplies a zero z0 with Re z0>0. Choose f in C_c^infinity(R) such that F_f(z0) and F_f(z0^#) are nonzero. For example choose a nonnegative smooth compact supported bump with integral one, rescale to support shrinking to 0; both transforms tend to 1 by dominated convergence. Fix one such f. Set c_z=m_z conj(F_f(z^#))F_f(z); c is absolutely summable and c_z0!=0. For real s, literal shift tau_s f(x)=f(x-s) gives
phi(s)=Q(f,tau_s f)=sum_z c_z exp(s z).
This series converges uniformly on bounded s intervals, since |Re z|<=1/2. Q(tau_s f,tau_s f)=Q(f,f) exactly because conj(z^#)+z=0. Both vectors fit some finite common centered aperture for each finite s.

Assume global nonnegativity at all finite apertures. Then by Cauchy-Schwarz for the positive semidefinite form, |phi(s)|<=Q(f,f) for every real s. The Laplace transform L(λ)=integral_0^infty exp(-λs)phi(s) ds is holomorphic for Re λ>0 by dominated convergence on compact sub-half-planes (also for its derivatives). For Re λ>1/2, absolute termwise integration is justified by sum|c_z|/(Re λ-1/2)<infinity, hence
L(λ)=sum_z c_z/(λ-z).
The series on the right is meromorphic in Re λ>0, with possible simple poles at right-half shifted zero points. Proof: on any compact set avoiding these points, distances to the discrete set are uniformly bounded below; local finiteness follows from the two-sided count, and for large |Im z| distances tend uniformly to infinity. Absolute summability of c gives normal convergence; near z0 isolate its term and the remainder is holomorphic. Its residue is c_z0!=0, since distinct zero indexing aggregates all identical multiplicity copies with the same sign. The right half-plane minus this discrete set is connected (join any two points by a compact path and detour finitely many points). Identity theorem extends L=series there. But L is holomorphic also at z0; impossible nonzero residue. Thus global positivity excludes every right-half shifted zero; reflection excludes every left-half shifted zero. This is RH. This proof does not require importing an additional equivalence of test classes: it uses only actual compactly supported smooth f and its finite shifts. It is a direct version of the Weil-criterion implication, not claimed novel in literature.

Stronger constructive statement conditional on an off-line zero: for the chosen bump, phi is unbounded on s>=0, by the same Laplace contradiction without assuming global positivity. Choose finite s>0 with |phi(s)|>|Q(f,f)|. The two-vector matrix on f,tau_s f is [[Q(f,f),phi(s)],[conj(phi(s)),Q(f,f)]], with eigenvalues Q(f,f)±|phi(s)|. Its negative coordinate eigenvalue gives an explicit negative linear combination of the two translated bumps (not a computed numerical shift). Such a test lies in a finite aperture. No off-line zero, residue, or shift has actually been found; this is a conditional construction.

## 4. Actual differential rigidity obstruction at a hypothetical critical aperture
Let H_a be the zero-extended physical logarithmic domain; use D=d/dx distributional derivative on R (not derivative of a restriction with unaccounted boundary terms). Let N_a={f in H_a: Q(f,g)=0 for all g in H_a}; finite-dimensional by exact compact factor. For f in H_a with Df also in H_a, the compactly supported distribution identity F_(Df)(z)=-z F_f(z) holds: choose a smooth cutoff equal to 1 near the support and integrate by parts distributionally. Both transforms use genuine L1 functions, as both vectors are supported L2. For g in C_c^infinity((-a,a)), its derivative is in H_a. In the exact polarized divisor pairing, use conj(z^#)=-z to obtain
Q(Df,g)=-Q(f,Dg).
Both series absolutely converge separately by the bounded sampling theorem on the actual differentiated vectors. This is not termwise differentiation of an unbounded ordinate group on arbitrary vectors. If f in N_a and Df in H_a, the right side vanishes. Interior smooth density in H_a and continuity imply Df in N_a. Iteration gives D^j f in N_a whenever the successive zero-extended distribution derivatives up to j are in H_a. If D^d f in H_a, intermediate derivatives are in H_a by the Fourier weight estimate |r|^(2j)<=1+|r|^(2d), and distributional support is retained.

Let d=dim N_a>0. A nonzero f in N_a cannot have D^d f in H_a: otherwise f,Df,...,D^d f give d+1 vectors in a d-dimensional space. They are independent, since a relation after Fourier transform has polynomial in i r times Fhat_f(r)=0 a.e.; a nonzero polynomial vanishes on a finite set and Fhat_f is nonzero in L2. Contradiction. In particular N_a contains no nonzero C_c^infinity(R) function (even one with full aperture support). If d=1, a critical vector cannot have its zero-extended first derivative in H_a. More generally, proving N_a is contained in the domain of the zero-extended D generator would be sufficient for exclusion: invariance of finite-dimensional N_a under D gives all derivatives in H_a, impossible. This is an exact conditional arithmetic rigidity criterion, but no proof of that domain inclusion is supplied. The logarithmic norm only supplies log regularity, not one full derivative; endpoint roughness is a real obstacle. Do not replace zero-extended derivative by interior derivative or assume boundary traces or smoothness. Do not infer kernel regularity from compactness; the Fredholm factor supplies only finite dimensionality.
