## 1. Exact observable
Let G(x)=sum_{n<=x} Lambda(n)(x/n-1)-x log x+(1+EulerGamma)x. Let rho denote distinct nontrivial zeta zeros with multiplicities m_rho, and z=rho-1/2. The classical once-smoothed explicit formula for x>1 is
G(x)=log(2pi)+sum_rho m_rho x^rho/[rho(1-rho)]-sum_{k>=1} x^(-2k)/[2k(2k+1)].
Signs follow from residue -m_rho at a zero of -zeta'/zeta, the double pole at 1 giving x(log x-1-EulerGamma), the pole at 0 giving log(2pi), and the trivial zeros giving the displayed negative series. This invokes the classical explicit formula (not a new contour proof). Nontrivial zero series absolutely converges for each x by standard N(T)=O(T log T); no RH assumption.

## 2. Signed translation probe
Use RETURN conventions F_f(z)=integral f(u) exp(zu) du, z#=-conj(z), Q(f,g)=sum_z m_z conj(F_f(z#)) F_g(z), tau_t f(u)=f(u-t).
Take f(u)=exp(u/2) for u<=0, zero otherwise. This is L2 but NOT compact supported. For -1/2<Re z<1/2, F_f(z)=1/(1/2+z)=1/rho and conj(F_f(z#))=1/(1/2-z)=1/(1-rho). Define the spectral scalar Phi(t)=sum_z m_z exp(tz)/(rho(1-rho)), real and even by conjugation and reflection. Then exactly, for t>0,
Phi(t)=exp(-t/2)[G(exp t)-log(2pi)+sum_{k>=1} exp(-2kt)/(2k(2k+1))].
Equivalently the autocorrelation of f is exp(-|u|/2), with bilateral Laplace transform 1/(1/4-z^2). Thus the smoothed prime error is a single signed spectral correlation, up to explicit elementary corrections.

## 3. Compact approximation and determinant
For T>0 set f_T=f 1_{[-T,0]}. Its transform is (1-exp(-T rho))/rho; the reflected factor is (1-exp(-T(1-rho)))/(1-rho). Their product is bounded by 4/|rho(1-rho)| and converges pointwise to 1/[rho(1-rho)]. Hence Q(f_T,tau_t f_T) converges to Phi(t) uniformly on compact real t intervals by absolute summability and exp(|t|/2). The spectral sums alone justify this limit. f_T has jumps but finite log-weight Fourier energy (decay O(1/|r|) for fixed T); actual graph-domain pairing still needs the exact compact carrier/explicit-formula attachment, or separate smoothing with justified convergence. No noncompact arithmetic form domain asserted.
With global compact-form nonnegativity and that attachment, each two-shift matrix is PSD; its limit is [[Phi(0),Phi(t)],[Phi(t),Phi(0)]], so |Phi(t)|<=Phi(0). The classical logarithmic derivative identity gives Phi(0)=2+EulerGamma-log(4pi); this evaluation is optional and not needed for the bridge.
Conversely, if Phi is bounded for t>=0, its Laplace transform is holomorphic for Re lambda>0. On Re lambda>1/2 it equals sum_z m_z/[rho(1-rho)(lambda-z)]. Absolute summability and local finiteness imply normal convergence off the discrete poles on Re lambda>0; nonzero residues at every right-half shifted zero contradict analytic continuation. Reflection excludes left-half zeros. Thus boundedness, and in particular positivity of all the limiting two-shift determinants with nonnegative diagonal, suffices for RH. This is RETURN's already recorded Laplace argument specialized to a fixed probe, not an independent novelty claim. Given a hypothetical right-half zero, some finite t has |Phi(t)|>|Phi(0)|, so by compact approximation the corresponding two-shift determinant is negative for all sufficiently large T (subject to domain attachment). No such zero or t has been exhibited.

## 4. What the comparison does and does not establish
For zeta specialization, a one-sided G bound by x^(1/2+epsilon) on arbitrarily late [Y,Y^(3/2)] becomes Phi(t)<=exp(epsilon t)+O(exp(-t/2)) on [log Y, (3/2)log Y] (constant factors in a chosen Gm normalization must be retained). The existing windowed Landau theorem converts these inequalities, for arbitrarily small epsilon, to zero exclusion. Global Weil positivity controls a whole family of Gram matrices; this window criterion controls one scalar projection on selected intervals. There is no established direct transfer from the sparse one-sided ceiling to determinant nonnegativity without the zero-exclusion argument (or an equivalent analytic argument).
