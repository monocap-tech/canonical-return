# Standing owner directive — research incident preservation and reporting
Effective 2026-11-27 Pacific. Authority: explicit owner instruction to keep all records and order all investigations and regroups to document issues through the central incident record. Applies prospectively and to recoverable historical evidence.

## Scope and obligation
All investigations, all seven current REGROUP-001 lineages (NF618, IV-AM, IV106, CR-ALP, CR-INT, MASTER-RETURN, MASTER-RECON), all future investigations and all current/future regroups shall preserve and report research difficulties through the central [GOVERNANCE_INCIDENT_REGISTER.md](GOVERNANCE_INCIDENT_REGISTER.md). This is a standing reporting obligation, not a new mathematical grant. A regroup is itself subject to incident reporting, including failures of governance interventions, assignment, reconciliation, handoff and the reporting mechanism itself.

Preserve successful and unsuccessful research records, including negative results, abandoned routes, failed and inconclusive runs, exact diagnostics, failed recovery attempts, corrective interventions and their outcomes, source and input provenance, reproducibility materials, custody receipts and decision history. Preserve originals in the proper repository and branch, with immutable references in the central record; never silently erase, rewrite or replace historical evidence. If corrections are needed, append a linked correction or supersession. Retain relevant raw logs and outputs when available and permitted; protect credentials, sensitive personal information and restricted material, recording redactions and access limitations rather than publishing secrets. A missing original or inaccessible record must be disclosed, not reconstructed as fact. No deletion or retention-policy change is authorized by this directive.

## Reporting and routing
1. On discovery of an issue, capture the first meaningful diagnostic and context promptly, before repair when feasible. Assign a provisional unique key (lineage/operation/date/sequence), record recurrence and link any existing incident family G-01 through G-08; do not force a novel failure into an existing category. Record distinct incidents and materially different attempts, even when they share a cause. Repeated identical events may be consolidated with counts and raw evidence references where available.
2. Use the existing register's incident entry contract: expected/actual behavior, exact refs and toolchain, trigger/run/job IDs when available, affected dependency graphs, downstream effect, measured cost or unknown, custody and evidence status, attempted remedy, result, remaining uncertainty and transferable lesson. Distinguish mathematical, execution, platform, custody, governance and adoption status. A hypothesis is not a verified cause; a mitigation is not a proven fix without evidence.
3. Store source evidence under existing research jurisdiction. Submit an incident entry or immutable local report link to the program governor; the central governance register is the canonical index, not a reason to relocate mathematical source. Owner authorizes reporting and the governor's central append-only administrative writes; no blanket cross-repository source mutation or branch/runner activation. Investigators may append directly only when their scoped authority and safe write route explicitly permit it; otherwise submit to governor with exact source pin. Governor serializes central updates with current blob/head checks, deduplicates without losing distinct occurrences and verifies remote readback. If no safe write route exists, mark reporting pending and preserve available evidence provisionally; never claim central custody.
4. At initialization/handoff read this directive and the relevant register entries (not the entire research history). At every operation terminal report and regroup checkpoint include new incident IDs, updates to existing IDs, pending submissions, and an explicit no-new-incidents statement if applicable. A regroup inventories unresolved incidents, reconciles references, identifies cross-lineage patterns and records dispositions and owners. At successor handoff carry the latest applicable directive pin and open incident refs. Do not infer permission to progress from incident reporting.
5. Retrospective backfill: each investigation and regroup indexes recoverable prior incidents from pinned records, identifying missing evidence, duplicates, and report-derived versus independently verified facts. Backfill is bounded by existing authorization and recovery limits; no wholesale re-execution or reopened retired searches merely to populate the register. No invented completeness claims.

## Dissemination and enforcement boundary
The program governor shall include this standing directive in all future initialization, continuation and regroup instructions, and relay it to all active lineages at the next authorized contact. Each recipient shall acknowledge the reporting obligation and identify the route for its incident submissions. Track acknowledgement separately from actual compliance. This document is an owner-mandated instruction in repository custody; it is not a message automatically delivered to separate existing chats and not installed technical enforcement. No CI dispatch, new branch, mathematical acceptance, adoption, release of holds, workflow modification, or change to prior mathematical jurisdiction is authorized. Existing more restrictive execution/custody rules remain in force. The immutable W02 entrypoint and prior reports remain historical; supply this directive alongside them.

## Regroup report minimum
- Incident register ref and readback status
- New/updated incident IDs and evidence pins
- Pending/unverified entries and acknowledgement gaps
- Open incident owners, disposition, and next authorized action or stop
- Explicit statement of no new issues if none observed; never equate absence of report with absence of incident

The register is empirical governance research evidence as well as an operational control record. Preserve the failures of the method, including failures of proposed remedies, so that later investigators can evaluate and reproduce the conditions under which they occurred. No claim is made that every implementation necessarily experiences the same failures.
