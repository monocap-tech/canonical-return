## 1. Fixed-width premise
Take 0<d<1/2, theta=1/2+d. Assume the fixed-exponent prime-window bound G(x)<=C*x^theta throughout [Y,Y^(3/2)] for arbitrarily large Y, and exact normalization identification above. Apply the cited external window theorem at theta only. Functional-equation reflection then yields |Re z|<=d for every shifted nontrivial zero z=rho-1/2. Alternatively take this strip bound directly as hypothesis. This uses zero exclusion at theta, NOT the final RH theorem; does not independently prove Landau or bypass its argument. No conclusion inside the width d strip.

## 2. A positive comparison kernel
Let z=b+i g, c_z=m_z/(1/4-z^2)=p_z+i q_z. Then A=1/4-b^2+g^2>0, B=2 b g, c_z=m_z(A+i B)/(A^2+B^2), p_z>0. Put r_d=d/sqrt(1/4-d^2). The elementary inequality 2 sqrt(1/4-d^2)|g| <= 1/4-d^2+g^2 gives |q_z|/p_z=|B|/A<=r_d. All sums converge absolutely by the usual zero count and bounded strip. Reflection and conjugation preserve multiplicity. Define Phi(t)=sum c_z exp(tz), as in COMPARATIVE-01. It is real and even, and Phi(0)=sum p_z=:S>0 (classical value 2+EulerGamma-log(4pi), not needed).
Define K(t)=sum p_z cos(g t), a positive-definite kernel: for any finite real t_i and complex a_i, sum conjugate(a_i) K(t_i-t_j) a_j >=0, since it is a positive sum of rank-one Gram kernels of e^(+/- i g t_i). Repeated zeros/ordinates cause no problem. No assumption that K is the actual zero divisor of a different L-function.
By pairing z with -conj z, which has b reversed, g unchanged, c conjugated, and taking real parts,
Phi(t)=sum [p_z cosh(b t) cos(g t) - q_z sinh(b t) sin(g t)].
The formula also holds for fixed points b=0 and for any real zero (g=0). Consequently
|Phi(t)-K(t)| <= S E_d(|t|),
E_d(D)=cosh(d D)-1+r_d sinh(d D).
The evenness of Phi follows from conjugation and reflection; K(0)=Phi(0)=S. This estimate is uniform in t on bounded intervals; no numerical zero approximations needed.

## 3. Finite Gram consequence
Take arbitrary finite real shifts t_1,...,t_n of diameter D=max |t_i-t_j|. Let M_ij=Phi(t_i-t_j), H_ij=K(t_i-t_j). H is PSD, M-H has diagonal zero and entry bounds S E_d(D). For a in C^n:
a* M a >= -S E_d(D) [(sum |a_i|)^2 - sum |a_i|^2] >= -(n-1) S E_d(D) ||a||_2^2.
Thus M+(n-1) S E_d(D) I is PSD. This is a finite shift-space coordinate estimate, not an L2 coercivity bound on arbitrary physical test functions. If H has certified min eigenvalue h, M>= [h-(n-1) S E_d(D)]I; no h asserted. For n=2, |Phi(t)|<= S(1+E_d(|t|)), determinant >= -S^2(2E_d+E_d^2). If the window premise holds for every d>0, taking d->0 for each fixed D,n yields M PSD and the limiting two-vector determinant nonnegative. For fixed D, E_d(D)=O(d^2) as d->0; leading coefficient D^2/2+2D. This does not bound how early a valid prime window begins or provide strict positivity. The entire spectral Gram statement uses the limiting exponential probe, not the full compact physical carrier; COMPARATIVE-01 truncation converges on bounded t, with arithmetic attachment still conditional.

## 4. Interpretation and limits
This gives a quantitative fixed-exponent comparison path: late prime windows -> strip control (external Landau theorem) -> explicit signed probe Gram defect bound -> exact limiting probe PSD as d tends to zero. It does not call the final RH theorem, but it DOES use its central analytical mechanism (zero exclusion), and all-width limit has RH strength. No direct route from window to determinant bypassing that mechanism established. The abstract implication from arbitrary even continuous one-sided ceiling to PSD is false: Phi_fake(t)=2-cosh(t), Phi_fake(0)=1, is always <=1 yet unbounded below and has negative two-point determinants for large |t|. This toy violates actual zeta residue/strip restrictions, so is not a counterexample to the real criterion. It merely illustrates why a raw one-sided ceiling alone is insufficient absent structural hypotheses. No assertion that the Gram defect result is new in the literature.
