## 2. Exact mixed trace-class lemma — resolves the first T1A gate

**Lemma (direct from §8.2's commutator calculus).** For *any* complex \(f,g\in\mathcal D_\lambda\), \(\theta(f)B_S\theta(g)^*\) is trace class on \(\mathcal H_S\); thus \(\mathfrak T_S(f,g)=\operatorname{Tr}(\theta(f)B_S\theta(g)^*)\) is well-defined, Hermitian, **linear first / antilinear second**.

**Proof.** In Mellin coordinates write \(F(t)=\mathcal Mf(it)\), \(G(t)=\mathcal Mg(it)\); both are Schwartz. Let \(U=M_u\) be the **unimodular semilocal local-factor ratio multiplier**, and \(H=2P-1\) as in the paper. By (53)/(59), \(B_S=\frac12 U^{-1}[H,U]\). Therefore

\[\boxed{\theta(f)B_S\theta(g)^*=\frac12M_F\bigl(U^{-1}[H,M_{u\overline G}]-[H,M_{\overline G}]\bigr).}\]

The derivatives of \(u(t)\) have at most tempered growth, so \(\overline G\) and \(u\overline G\) are Schwartz and extend smoothly to the Cayley compactification. **Lemma 8.3(i)** says their commutators with \(H\) are trace class (Fourier coefficients satisfy \(\sum|n||\varphi_n|<\infty\)); multiplication by bounded \(F,U^{-1}\) preserves trace class. This proves the claim **without any reality or pole-null condition**. Notice that \(B_S\) itself is generally *not* trace class, so this smoothing argument is essential.

By standard trace cyclicity for bounded products whose two orderings are trace class (the same argument for \(\theta(f*g^*)B_S\), using \(\mathcal M(f*g^*)=F\overline G\)),

\[\boxed{\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\theta(f)B_S\theta(g)^*\bigr)=\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\theta(f*g^*)B_S\bigr).}\]

This is a **real mathematical source-domain result**: the individual *mixed* trace is defined, not merely the sum of two mixed traces obtained by real polarization.
